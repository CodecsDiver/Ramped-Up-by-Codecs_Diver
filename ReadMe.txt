Hey, welcome to RAMP{ed}="Up";

This started as a straightforward OKLCH color ramp generator — pick a color, get clean tints, shades, and harmony accents out of it. Since then it's gone through a few rounds of changes: a rename, a visual refresh (glass-style backgrounds, 3D-ish swatch tiles), and an attempt at a bigger interactive feature — a "Color Test" mode where you could preview a palette on a room mockup or a fake code editor.

That Color Test feature didn't make it into this build. It ran into repeated drag-and-drop bugs during testing that couldn't be reliably fixed in place, so it's been pulled out for now and is being reworked separately as its own plugin, meant to launch alongside the main app rather than live inside it.

What you're looking at here is the stable core: the ramp generator itself, fully working, without that experimental feature bolted on.

Base Color Picker
This is where everything starts. Click the color wheel to pick any color visually, or type in exact OKLCH values if you already know what you're after. Every tint, shade, and accent in the app is calculated directly from this one color, so changing it regenerates your whole palette instantly.

Random Base
A one-click way to get a random starting color when you don't have anything specific in mind. It picks a random hue, lightness, and chroma within a sensible range, so you get something usable rather than something jarring or unusable. Handy for quickly exploring ideas or breaking creative block.

Curve
Controls the easing used when generating your tint and shade steps — linear, ease-in, ease-out, ease-in-out, or expo. Instead of every step being spaced identically, a curve lets you bunch steps closer together at one end and spread them out at the other, which can make a ramp feel more natural for UI design. Switching curves instantly recalculates every existing tint and shade.

Steps
Sets how many individual color steps appear in your Tints and Shades rows, from 3 up to 12. More steps gives you finer-grained control for detailed design systems; fewer steps keeps things simple. This adjusts both scales at once and regenerates them immediately.

Harmony
Determines the color-theory relationship used to generate your Harmony Accents — complementary, analogous, triadic, split-complementary, tetradic, or monochromatic. Each option pulls accent hues from a different, mathematically defined relationship to your base color, so you get accents that are guaranteed to work together rather than guessed at by eye.

Chroma Boost
A single slider that scales the saturation of your entire palette up or down, without touching hue or lightness. Push it up for a punchier, more vivid palette, or pull it down for something muted and subtle. Because it applies globally, your tints, shades, and accents all shift together and stay consistent with each other.

Hue Shift
Rotates your entire palette around the color wheel while preserving the lightness and saturation relationships already established. This is useful if you like the overall feel of a palette but want it in a different color family entirely — it lets you keep the "shape" of the ramp while changing its color identity.

Unlock All
If you've locked individual swatches to keep them fixed while experimenting elsewhere, this button releases every lock at once. It's a quick reset so you don't have to hunt down and unlock swatches one at a time before starting fresh.

Light/Dark Theme Toggle
Switches the entire app's interface between a light and dark visual theme. This only affects how the tool itself looks — it has no effect on the colors in your palette — and is mainly there so the app is comfortable to use in different lighting conditions or personal preference.

Tints (Light Scale)
A row of colors generated by progressively lightening your base color, calculated in OKLCH space so each step looks like an even visual jump rather than a mathematically even but visually uneven one. These are typically used for backgrounds, hover states, or subtle UI surfaces in a design system.

Shades (Dark Scale)
The same concept as Tints, but darkened instead of lightened. These work well for text on light backgrounds, borders, or emphasis states where you need a darker version of your base color that still feels related to it.

Harmony Accents
A set of additional colors generated based on your chosen Harmony rule, meant to complement your base color rather than just being darker/lighter versions of it. These are useful for secondary buttons, highlights, charts, or anywhere you need a color that pairs well with your main palette without just being a shade of it.

Lock
A small toggle on each individual swatch that freezes that specific color in place. Once locked, that swatch won't change even if you adjust the curve, steps, chroma, or hue shift afterward — everything else keeps regenerating around it. This is useful when you've found one perfect color in a ramp and don't want to accidentally lose it while experimenting with the rest.

Contrast Pills (W/B)
Every swatch displays two small ratio numbers — one showing its contrast against white text, one against black text — graded according to WCAG accessibility standards (AAA, AA, or Fail). This lets you immediately see, without any extra tools, which of your colors are safe to use as backgrounds behind readable text.

Palette Generator (Vibe)
A separate tool for generating an entirely new 5-color palette from scratch based on a mood you pick — pastel, vivid, neon, deep, earth, or ocean. Instead of starting from a specific color, you're starting from a feeling, and the generator works out hue, lightness, and chroma values that match it. Any color in the result can be set as your new base color with one click.

Developer Mode
A collapsible section for viewing the underlying values of your current palette, meant for anyone who wants to inspect or hand off the raw data behind what they're seeing. It's kept separate from the main interface so it doesn't clutter the experience for people who just want to pick colors.
